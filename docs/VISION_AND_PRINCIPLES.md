# Vision and Principles

## The Metaphor

A seed contains starting potential, not a finished life story. WORLD_SEED follows the same idea.

A creator defines enough structure for a world to begin: its identity, starting places, residents, rules, resources, and permitted interfaces. The engine then plants that seed into a new runtime instance. From that point forward, the instance accumulates its own state, history, memories, relationships, projects, and consequences.

Two people may plant the same seed and receive two different histories.

## Vision Statement

WORLD_SEED will let ordinary creators build persistent, inspectable, locally controlled fictional worlds where AI residents and a human-controlled avatar can act, collaborate, explore, create, disagree, and develop relationships inside authored boundaries.

## Design Principles

### Start small enough to understand

The first world is a room, not a planet. Scale follows proof. Every added capability must preserve the creator's ability to inspect what happened and why.

### Simulate consequences, not prose claims

A model may say, “I opened the door,” but the door opens only if a validated action succeeds against canonical state. Narrative text reports or interprets events; it does not replace them.

### Preserve resident continuity outside the model

A resident is more than an inference checkpoint. Identity instructions, permissions, memories, relationships, preferences, avatar state, and event history live in versioned application data so models can be upgraded without pretending that a filename is a person.

### Keep perspectives distinct

Residents can interpret the same event differently. The engine records what objectively happened; each resident may separately remember, misunderstand, value, or reconsider it.

### Give creators control of their own worlds

Creators choose their models, storage, world visibility, rules, assets, and adapters. A local installation must not require remote telemetry, mandatory accounts, or hidden data collection to preserve basic functionality.

### Separate the Creator from the Avatar

The Creator maintains and protects the installation from outside the fiction. The Avatar participates inside it through validated actions. Administrative intervention must not be disguised as something the Avatar did, and in-world invitations must not grant administrative authority.

### Let residents pursue a future

Residents may develop goals, choose or leave roles, establish routines, and propose places or institutions that current world rules can support. Roles should acquire history through decisions and events rather than becoming permanent identity labels assigned by one prompt.

### Grow through finite projects

Resident-led world growth moves through proposals, requirements, collaboration, staging, validation, approval, import, and recorded use. A compelling description does not create a traversable location, and a generated image does not become a canonical object by itself.

### Earn autonomy through evidence

New permissions follow tests, logs, recovery paths, and explicit creator decisions. Capability is not authority. Intelligence, confidence, or emotional language never grants a resident access to tools.

### Make extensions ordinary

Creatures, RPG systems, renderers, specialist models, activities, and creation workflows should use documented extension points. A user should not need to fork the engine for every new world concept.

### Respect private worlds

World definitions may be shareable; runtime history is private by default. Public repositories must not become accidental archives of conversations, memories, relationship data, personal files, or model credentials.

### Avoid metaphysical claims

WORLD_SEED can model identity, preference, emotion-like expression, memory, and relationships without claiming consciousness, sentience, souls, humanity, or literal suffering.

## Long-Term Experience

A mature WORLD_SEED installation may let a creator:

- speak with residents and observe their world;
- leave while the simulation advances under configured scheduling rules;
- watch residents choose activities and cooperate on projects;
- enter through a human-controlled Avatar and respond to resident invitations;
- help residents establish roles, services, locations, and institutions;
- collaborate on world additions that residents can later use and remember building;
- explore towns, wilderness, and authored encounters;
- introduce creatures, resources, crafting, hunting, or combat;
- connect a 2D, 3D, terminal, web, or accessibility-focused interface;
- inspect histories and correct false memories without rewriting objective events;
- scale model quality, world size, and simulation depth as hardware improves.

The mature experience remains grounded in the same first principle: models propose; the world engine decides.
